# Open Questions and Assumptions

Status: Draft v1 — please review before Milestone 0 begins. Items here are either explicit assumptions made to keep the architecture moving, or decisions that genuinely belong to the business/product owner, not engineering.

## 1. Decisions received from product owner (2026-07-22) — resolved

- **Tip prompt scope.** Ticket checkout never prompts for a tip. Tipping only ever appears in the restaurant/dining tab flow. Implemented in PAYMENT_FLOW.md §3/§4.
- **Showtime end time & auto-settlement timing.** No end time is manually entered or shown to customers. `Showtime.endsAt` is computed as `startsAt + movie.runtimeMinutes` (runtime is treated as running through the credits). After a showtime ends: 15 minutes for cleaning, then the next showtime's pre-show window (15 minutes for customer entry/ordering + 15 minutes of trailers) before its start — a fixed 45-minute auditorium turnover, enforced when scheduling showtimes. Auto-settlement fires shortly after `endsAt` (default 5-minute grace), because the tab must be fully settled before the cleaning window is needed for the next audience. Detailed in DATA_MODEL.md's new "Showtime scheduling & turnover" section and PAYMENT_FLOW.md §6.
- **Refund policy.** Full refunds only (100%) for MVP — no partial-refund workflow, no dollar-threshold escalation tier. Any role with the `payment.refund` permission can issue a full refund directly. See SECURITY.md §2.1 footnote and PAYMENT_FLOW.md §7.
- **Screening-cancellation comp policy.** Confirmed: comped. Canceled showtimes automatically refund all tickets and comp (write off, or refund if already charged) any open/delivered restaurant tabs for that showtime — no per-incident staff judgment call required. See RESTAURANT_WORKFLOW.md §8.
- **Split-tender on restaurant payments.** Confirmed acceptable as designed: multiple `Payment` rows against one tab (e.g., one cash, one card), summing to the total. This is standard practice among restaurant POS systems (Toast, Square, and Micros all model split tender the same way — separate tender lines against one check, not one blended transaction), so no redesign was needed.
- **Membership program.** Confirmed lookup/attach stub only for MVP; a real program is a future phase, not required now.
- **Tab settlement is staff-driven (check drop), not silently automatic.** Confirmed (2026-07-25), superseding the earlier "automatic settlement" framing in item 2 above: real service is servers dropping checks roughly 30 minutes before a showtime ends (configurable), guests still allowed one more order after that, and the server's finalize/payment-collection action being what actually closes the tab — including splitting payment across multiple cards, not requiring everything land on the card used at ticket checkout. The previously-described automatic job still exists, but now strictly as a fallback for a tab whose check never got dropped, not the primary experience. See PAYMENT_FLOW.md §6.1/§6.2, STATE_MACHINES.md §5, RESTAURANT_WORKFLOW.md §6.1.
- **Business structure of Meridian (cinema) and Afterglow (bar).** Confirmed (2026-07-25): one company, not two separate legal entities — Afterglow isn't a standalone bar business that happens to share a building with Meridian, it's the same bar station that both runs its own walk-in tabs (RESTAURANT_WORKFLOW.md §10) and fulfills in-seat drink orders for moviegoers during films via the existing seat-linked ordering/`KitchenStation(type=BAR)` routing (RESTAURANT_WORKFLOW.md §4, §6) — the two roles are operationally inseparable, unlike, say, a landlord-tenant bar sharing a lobby. This matches how Nitehawk Cinema and its bar Lo-Res are structured (same company, one ownership), not a hypothetical. Consequence for the platform: Afterglow needs no special modeling of its own — it's represented as the bar `KitchenStation` (and, if it ever gets its own numbered seating, optionally its own small seat map) inside Meridian's single `Organization`/`Location`, not a second tenant with its own Stripe Connect account. If this ever changes (e.g., Afterglow spins out as its own legal entity later, commonly done specifically to isolate liquor liability), it would become a second `Organization` at that point — a real migration, not a config flip, so worth getting this decision right now rather than assuming it's easily reversible.
- **Business model, superseding the earlier "years out" framing above.** Confirmed (2026-07-24): this is being built as a real multi-tenant SaaS product from day one, not an internal tool that might someday be generalized. The strategy is to undercut expensive incumbents (Vista, Filmbot) on price for small independent theaters — viable because AI-assisted development gives this a structurally lower cost basis than a funded competitor — with the resulting revenue intended to help fund the founder's own separate cinema venture. The founder's own theater is Organization/tenant #1, onboarded through the same path as any other customer, not special-cased. See PRODUCT_SPEC.md §1.1 and PAYMENT_FLOW.md §1.1 for the architectural consequences (per-tenant Stripe Connect payment routing, in particular).

## 1.1a Distributor splits and Comscore reporting — deferred, not blocking, reasoning confirmed 2026-07-25

No distributor or booker relationship exists yet (hypothetical/pre-launch), so this is deliberately deferred rather than built now — but the reasoning for *why* it's safe to defer is worth recording:

- **How theatrical settlement actually works, confirmed via industry sources:** the exhibitor (theater) collects the full box office gross at time of sale, then remits the distributor's contractually agreed share afterward as a periodic "settlement," not a real-time split at the moment of purchase. Splits are typically a sliding scale by week (a bigger cut to the distributor early in a film's run, more kept by the theater the longer it plays — roughly 55% to the distributor in week one down to 30% in later weeks for wide studio releases; independent/specialty distributors are commonly lower, around 28-35%), sometimes with a "house nut" (a minimum operating-cost deduction the theater takes before the split applies). None of this happens at the register — it's a downstream accounting relationship.
- **Merchant-of-record responsibility already sits with the theater, by design.** Because payments route via Stripe Connect directly to each theater's own connected account rather than pooling through the platform operator (PAYMENT_FLOW.md §1.1), the theater — not this software — is the Merchant of Record for every ticket and F&B sale. Sales tax collection/remittance, and by the same logic distributor payment remittance and any required Comscore reporting, are legally the theater's obligations, not the platform's. This isn't a gap to close; it's a direct, favorable consequence of a decision already made.
- **What the platform's job actually is here:** provide accurate underlying data (already covered — per-movie/per-showtime revenue and ticket counts, IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md Milestone 10), and, if and when a real distributor relationship requires it, build a specific export in whatever format that reporting channel needs. Actually calculating or remitting money owed to a distributor is a bookkeeping/accounting action outside the ticketing system, the same pattern already used for payroll (hours exported, not processed in-house).
- **Revisit when:** a real booking/distribution relationship exists and its contract terms (and whether Comscore or an equivalent reporting channel is actually required) are known. Nothing here blocks current milestones.

## 1.1 New questions raised by the confirmed SaaS business model

These are genuinely undecided and don't block early milestones (they matter starting around Milestone 3, when real payments are built), but should be resolved before that point:

- **Pricing structure** — flat monthly subscription per theater, a small per-transaction application fee (Stripe Connect supports this natively), or a hybrid? "Cheap and easy to adopt" was the stated goal; the specific number and structure aren't decided.
- **Does the founder's own theater pay the platform, or is it comped?** Either is defensible (comped as the pilot/dogfooding customer vs. paying real fees to validate the pricing model works even on the friendliest possible customer) — worth a deliberate choice rather than a default.
- **Onboarding flow ownership** — Stripe Connect's hosted onboarding (fastest to build, Stripe's UI) vs. a custom-branded onboarding flow (more work, more control over first impression for a theater signing up). Reasonable to start with Stripe-hosted and revisit once there's a real second customer.
- **Support model for other theaters** — once someone besides the founder is a paying customer, someone needs to answer their questions when something breaks. Not a blocker for building, but worth having an answer before actively selling to a second theater.

## 2. Recommendations (pending your confirmation, not blocking current work)

- **Hosting.** Recommended: Vercel for the four Next.js frontends, and Railway (or Render as a close second) for the NestJS API, PostgreSQL, and Redis. Reasoning: minimal operational burden for a small team, inexpensive to start, both are Docker-compatible so a later move to AWS/GCP — relevant if the multi-location/SaaS ambition above materializes — is a re-platform, not a rewrite. This doesn't need to be locked in until Milestone 11.
- **Email provider.** Recommended: Postmark — strong transactional deliverability, simple API, good for receipts/confirmations. Sits behind the existing `EmailProvider` abstraction either way.
- **SMS provider.** Recommended: Twilio, the de facto standard. Worth confirming whether SMS is actually needed for MVP launch (order-ready pings, settlement failure alerts) or can wait — it adds cost and a second provider integration for a channel email may cover adequately at first.
- **Payroll provider.** Confirmed (2026-07-24): native PIN-based time clock is in scope (Milestone 9), matching Vista and Toast, both of which build clock-in/out into their core platform. Actual payroll — tax withholding, filing, direct deposit — is explicitly out of scope and won't be built in-house, matching how those same platforms hand payroll off to a dedicated product (Toast Payroll) or a third-party integration (Gusto, ADP, Netchex, etc.). No specific payroll provider needs to be chosen now; the design commitment is just that `Shift` data (DATA_MODEL.md) stays exportable (CSV now, API integration later) so a provider can be plugged in whenever it's needed.

## 3. Assumptions made to keep the architecture concrete

- Currency: USD. Locale: US sales-tax model (location-configurable flat rates per tax category, not an external tax-calculation service) and English-only UI for MVP.
- Payment processor: Stripe, test/sandbox mode throughout development, behind the `PaymentProvider` abstraction so this is revisitable without a rewrite.
- Auditorium layouts authored via structured configuration (rows/seats/attributes as data), not a graphical drag-and-drop designer, for MVP — per the spec's own permission to make this trade-off if a graphical builder would delay the core system.
- Native mobile apps are out of scope for MVP; APIs are designed to be reusable by a future native client but none is built now.
- Apple/Google Wallet integration is architected for (ticket data model supports it) but not implemented in MVP.
- "One theater location, three auditoriums" is the literal MVP deployment target; the data model supports more without a schema change.
- Tax and service-charge calculation is a configurable-rules engine at the rate level, not a full tax-jurisdiction determination service — appropriate for a single physical location, would need revisiting for multi-location/multi-jurisdiction.
- No legal, tax, alcohol-licensing, or accounting requirement has been independently researched or asserted as fact anywhere in this documentation set; every place the system touches these domains, it exposes configuration rather than encoding a specific jurisdiction's rule as if it were universally true. Confirm applicable requirements with qualified counsel/accountant before go-live.
- PCI: SAQ A scope is targeted via tokenization (SECURITY.md §3); this is not a claim of compliance, only a scope-reduction design choice.

## 5. Explicitly flagged risks

- **Settlement risk is now split across two different failure modes, both still real money-movement.** The primary path (check drop, PAYMENT_FLOW.md §6.1) has a human in the loop, which lowers the risk of a silent bad charge, but introduces a new one: staff manually entering a split across multiple cards is a manual-entry error surface (wrong amount to the wrong card, double-collecting a portion) that a pure background job never had — this needs its own test coverage, not just a reuse of the old automatic-settlement tests. The fallback path (§6.2) keeps the original risk profile — money moving with no human in the loop — and still deserves the most thorough testing and a staged rollout before relying on it unattended, but it should now be firing rarely (only when staff genuinely never dropped a check), which is itself worth monitoring: a fallback path that fires often means check-drop isn't being followed operationally, which is a training/process problem worth surfacing, not just a technical one.
- **Turnover scheduling is now a hard validation, not a soft suggestion.** Because both check-drop timing and the fallback settlement job's timing depend on it, a bug in the showtime-overlap validation (DATA_MODEL.md) doesn't just risk a double-booked auditorium — it risks staff being prompted to drop checks at the wrong time, or the fallback job's assumptions about when a room needs to be clear. Cover this validation with tests as carefully as seat concurrency.
- **In-person card collection — RESOLVED 2026-07-25.** Confirmed: Stripe Terminal (physical PCI-validated card readers paired to `staff-pos` devices), not a repurposing of the online tokenized-entry flow. See PAYMENT_FLOW.md §1.2 for the interface extension, SECURITY.md §3 for the resulting (separate) PCI scope story, and IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md Milestones 8-9 for where it's built. Reader hardware is a real recurring cost worth folding into the pricing-model decision below, not just an engineering line item.
- **QR ticket tokens must be unguessable and signed**, not sequential ids — implemented as such (SEAT-to-QR design in Milestone 4), flagged here because a guessable ticket token would allow ticket forgery/scan fraud.
- **Webhook endpoint is an internet-facing endpoint that triggers financial state changes** — signature verification (SECURITY.md §5) is the entire defense; this must never be weakened or bypassed for debugging convenience, including in staging.
- **Role/permission definitions living in code, not admin-editable**, is a deliberate anti-privilege-escalation choice (DATA_MODEL.md, Employee/Role/Permission section) — flagging so it's not "fixed" later by someone adding a permission-editor UI without recognizing why it was avoided.
