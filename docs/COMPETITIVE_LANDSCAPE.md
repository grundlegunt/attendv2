# Competitive Landscape & Integration Reality

Status: Summary, informal — captures the industry research and reasoning behind several product decisions in PRODUCT_SPEC.md and OPEN_QUESTIONS.md. Not a formal spec section.

## How the industry actually works

The core mechanic behind every reserved-seat ticketing system, whether it's a single indie theater or a chain sold on Fandango, is the same: there is exactly one authoritative record of which seats are sold, and every sales channel has to check and lock against that single record in real time. Fandango doesn't keep its own separate seat inventory for a chain like Regal — when you buy a seat through the Fandango app, it's transacting live against Regal's own backend reservation system, the same one Regal's own app and website use. Fandango, the chain's app, and the chain's website are three front doors into one room. That's the only way double-booking becomes structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely, and it's the same principle behind this platform's own seat-hold design (database row locking plus a hard uniqueness constraint on active tickets).

## The three companies we've been comparing against

**Vista** is the enterprise end of the market — sold to large multiplex chains, and genuinely comprehensive. It handles ticketing, POS, and even staff time & attendance natively (their "Vista Time" module lives inside the core cinema system, not bolted on). It's expensive and built for scale, which is exactly the gap an independent-theater-focused product can undercut.

**Filmbot** sits at the lean, independent-cinema end. It covers ticketing, box office, cinema website management, and integrations like Stripe, TheMovieDB, Fandango listings, and Comscore box-office reporting — but deliberately does not do staff scheduling, time clock, or payroll. That's a sensible scope choice for them: scheduling is a solved, generic problem with an entire mature market of cheap point solutions already serving every restaurant and retail business, so building it themselves would mean competing with specialists at something that isn't their differentiator.

**Nitehawk** isn't a software vendor at all — it's a real operating dine-in chain (Brooklyn), useful here as a case study in how small operators actually run things day to day. Their in-seat food ordering has historically been a low-tech "flag system" (paper and a physical flag, no app involved), and public evidence (their job listings are hosted on `jobs.7shifts.com`) suggests they run staff scheduling through 7shifts, a dedicated third-party scheduling app, separate from whatever runs their box office. In other words, a real independent theater today is likely stitching together two or three point solutions rather than running one unified system — which is exactly the gap this platform is aimed at closing, cheaply.

**Toast** (restaurant POS, not cinema, but the closest analog for the dining side) follows the same split as Vista: time clock is native to the core POS, but actual payroll — tax withholding, filing, direct deposit — is a separate product line (Toast Payroll) or a third-party integration (Gusto, ADP, etc.), never folded into the core system.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vista | Yes | Limited | Native | Integrated | Large chains |
| Filmbot | Yes | No | No | No | Small indie theaters |

## What we're building

A single platform doing both halves properly — reserved-seat ticketing and full restaurant POS, with every food/drink order tied to the seat it was ordered from. That combination is closer to Vista's breadth than Filmbot's, but built at a structurally lower cost (AI-assisted development instead of a funded engineering team) so it can be priced for the same small independent theaters Filmbot targets, without asking them to also run a separate restaurant POS and a separate scheduling tool.

It's multi-tenant from day one: every theater, including the founder's own, is an Organization onboarded the same way, with its own Stripe Connect account so its revenue lands directly in its own bank account rather than pooling anywhere else. Features that aren't core to the pitch — like the time clock — are native but toggleable per theater, so a theater already happy with 7shifts or Homebase isn't stuck with an unused feature cluttering their screens. Payroll processing itself (tax withholding, filing, direct deposit) is deliberately not being built — hours are captured and exportable, matching how even Vista and Toast keep that piece separate or handed off to a specialist.

## Where the real API/integration hurdles are

The API that already exists — the backend our four apps (customer site, staff POS, kitchen display, admin) all talk to — isn't a hurdle at all; it's normal internal architecture, already built.

The actual challenge only shows up if a theater wants to keep some other system running instead of adopting ours as the system of record. Two distinct versions of that:

1. **Keeping a different restaurant POS** (Toast, Square, Micros) instead of ours. This would need a custom adapter per vendor — no shared standard exists between them — plus ongoing maintenance as each one changes its own API. Real, recurring engineering cost per integration, not a one-time task.
2. **Keeping a different box-office/ticketing system** while still wanting to appear on an aggregator like Fandango. Architecturally this is very achievable — Fandango (or any aggregator) would just become one more channel calling into the same seat-hold/locking mechanism already built for our own customer site. But actually getting listed on Fandango is a business-development and partner-certification process with Fandango itself, not a coding problem, and it's separate from anything we control.

The strategic read: chasing broad compatibility with every POS a prospective theater might already own cuts against the "cheap, one clean product" pitch — it's expensive engineering for a small number of theaters and works against the whole reason this can undercut Vista on price. Narrow, specific integrations (a payroll CSV export, Comscore box-office reporting, a QuickBooks export) are far more tractable than a general "plug into anything" layer, and match what Filmbot itself actually ships. The stronger sell stays: *this replaces your ticketing and your restaurant POS — nothing to integrate.*

## Validation note: Alamo Drafthouse needed a third-party company just to unify cinema + restaurant data

Alamo Drafthouse's customer-facing "Victory" is a loyalty program (like AMC A-List), not their underlying ticketing/POS technology. What's actually notable: Alamo brought in a separate company, Mirus (a restaurant data-warehouse/BI vendor), specifically because their cinema ticketing data and restaurant POS data lived in separate systems that didn't share data — their own IT director has stated analysts had to manually compile and merge the two before Mirus unified it for reporting. Real evidence that even a major, well-resourced dine-in chain ended up needing a downstream patch to answer questions the seat-to-tab chain here (DATA_MODEL.md §5) answers natively, at the source, without a third product in between.

## Gap analysis (2026-07-25) — what other platforms do that we don't yet

A closer look at Veezi (Vista's own indie-cinema tier — arguably the single closest real competitor to what's being built here, more so than either Vista's enterprise product or Filmbot), plus general industry practice at chains that offer group/private screenings, turned up a few real gaps worth naming honestly rather than assuming the current plan already covers everything.

**Walk-up sales with no seat attached — RESOLVED 2026-07-25.** Every payment in the schema was `purpose = TICKET_ORDER` or `RESTAURANT_TAB`, and every `RestaurantTab` required a `RestaurantTabSeat` — no path existed for a standalone bar or counter sale with no ticket at all. This became concrete once Meridian's own plan (a bar, Afterglow, serving pre/post-movie guests and walk-in customers off the street) made the gap non-hypothetical. Fixed by making `RestaurantTab.showtimeId`/`RestaurantOrder.showtimeSeatId` nullable and adding `RestaurantTab.tabType` (`SEAT_LINKED`/`WALK_IN`) — see DATA_MODEL.md §1/§2, RESTAURANT_WORKFLOW.md §10, and IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md Milestone 6. Same menu/routing/payment machinery either way; walk-in tabs just settle manually at the bar instead of through the showtime-triggered auto-settlement job.

**Distributor box-office reporting (Comscore or equivalent).** Comscore's Electronic Box Office Transaction Reporting is the standard industry mechanism for reporting grosses to studios and distributors — its own materials describe it as used by "every major movie studio... and prominent independent movie distributor." This may not be optional in practice: some film licensing/booking agreements can require it to book certain titles at all. It isn't in scope anywhere in the current docs. Worth a direct answer from whoever handles film booking/licensing for the theater (or a distributor rep) on whether this is actually required before first-run titles can be booked — flagging it in OPEN_QUESTIONS.md rather than silently assuming it's optional.

**Real membership/loyalty tiers, not just a lookup stub.** Current scope treats membership as attach/lookup only, deferred indefinitely (OPEN_QUESTIONS.md). Veezi, and smaller competitors like Omniterm and Anolla, treat this as an actual paid-tier recurring-revenue product — monthly/annual membership with perks like free tickets, F&B discounts, waived online fees, and early access to premieres. That's real money being left further out than it needs to be; worth reconsidering priority even if it doesn't move into the MVP itself.

**Gift cards and vouchers.** Explicitly out of scope currently. Veezi bundles this as a standard feature, and it's a low-complexity, high-margin, seasonal revenue line (a lot of gift card balances are never fully redeemed). Reasonable to reconsider as a near-term add rather than indefinitely deferred.

**Private screenings, theater buyouts, and group/school sales.** A real, commonly offered revenue line at both dine-in independents and major chains (Showcase Cinemas runs a dedicated buyout/group-screening booking flow) — renting a whole auditorium for a birthday, corporate event, or school field trip, usually with different pricing and a custom film-selection process rather than per-seat retail sale. Not represented anywhere in the current docs.

**Digital signage.** Veezi includes lobby/marquee display integration as standard. This is a comparatively easy win given the showtime data already lives in the API — likely just a simple read-only display endpoint/screen, not new architecture.

## Nonprofit / general-admission venue gap analysis (Belcourt case study, 2026-07-25)

Tested the plan against a real nonprofit arthouse — Belcourt Theatre, Nashville (501(c)(3), two screens, 300+ films/3,500+ screenings a year) — as a stand-in for a whole class of theater this platform currently doesn't fit well: nonprofit, general-admission, membership/donation-funded rather than dine-in-driven. What's actually needed:

**General admission ticketing mode — the big structural one.** Belcourt doesn't use a seat map or preselected seats. Every part of the current design that makes seat-linked dining possible (`ShowtimeSeat`, seat-hold locking, the paired-shared-table rendering) assumes a ticket maps to a specific seat, and none of it applies here. This needs a real second mode, not a workaround: a `Showtime`/`Auditorium` `seatingMode` of `RESERVED` or `GENERAL_ADMISSION`, where a GA showtime sells against a capacity count rather than individual seats — still needs the same concurrency discipline as seat holds (can't oversell a sold-out screening under concurrent checkouts), just simpler, a locked capacity decrement instead of a per-seat lock. This is worth building regardless of any one theater, since most small/older/community cinemas and nearly all film festivals run general admission, not reserved seating — it's a real expansion of who the platform can sell to, not a one-off accommodation.

**Real membership/giving-tier program, with the tax mechanics that come with it.** Belcourt's membership ($50/$40 annually, tiered, benefits increasing with the gift) is legally a charitable contribution with benefits attached, which triggers IRS quid pro quo contribution rules — a nonprofit must disclose the fair market value of what a donor received, and only the amount above that value is tax-deductible; a plain donation with no benefits is fully deductible. Nothing in the schema currently represents this. Needs: a real `MembershipTier`/`MembershipGift` concept (replacing the lookup-only stub), `Payment.purpose` gaining `DONATION`/`MEMBERSHIP_GIFT` alongside `TICKET_ORDER`/`RESTAURANT_TAB`, and receipt generation that states the deductible amount correctly — not just a generic order confirmation.

**A standalone donate flow**, not tied to buying a ticket at all — one-time and likely recurring/monthly giving, since that's standard for nonprofit development offices. Currently every payment in the system originates from a ticket or a tab; giving money with nothing purchased in return doesn't have a path.

**Confirm the whole restaurant/dine-in subsystem is optional at the Organization/Location level, not just time clock.** Belcourt's concessions (popcorn, snacks, beer, wine at a walk-up counter) map directly onto the walk-in/counter-sale flow already built for Afterglow — good news, no new work there — but a venue like this should be able to run with zero seat-linked dining machinery active at all, not just unused.

**Nonprofit-specific reporting.** Grant applications and board reporting typically need donor/membership reports and program-level attendance numbers, not just revenue-per-movie — an extension of the Milestone 10 reporting scope, not a new subsystem.

**Checked against PatronBase's actual product line (2026-07-25)**, since it's the closer real competitor for this segment: their offering spans CRM, eCommerce, Entry Manager, Fundraising, Marketing, Membership, QuickPOS, PatronPoints Loyalty, Seasons and Packages, Ticketing, and Venue Manager. Most of that maps onto ground already covered (ticketing, entry/scanning, POS, venue/seat-map config) or onto the membership/donation gap already named above. A few don't, and are worth naming as their own items rather than folding into "membership":

- **Season passes / packages** — the most concretely valuable one for a high-volume repertory venue. Buy once, redeem across many future showtimes (a flex pass, a festival package, a subscription series), rather than one ticket tied to one specific showtime at time of purchase. Nothing in the current `TicketOrder`/`Ticket` model supports this — every ticket is sold against a specific showtime directly. Genuinely well-suited to a theater doing 300+ films a year like Belcourt, and to film festivals generally.
- **Loyalty points, as a distinct thing from paid membership.** PatronBase treats "pay for a membership tier" and "earn points on purchases, redeem for rewards" as two separate products (Membership vs. PatronPoints Loyalty). The gap analysis above only covers the first. A transactional points system is a different mechanic and hasn't been considered at all yet.
- **Fundraising as real campaign management**, not just a donation record. PatronBase's fundraising module tracks multiple campaigns against targets with real-time progress, pledges/asks, and grant/restricted-fund reporting requirements and due dates. What's scoped above (a `Donation`/`MembershipGift` payment type with correct tax receipting) covers *taking* a donation correctly; it doesn't cover *campaign* tracking (a capital campaign toward a stated goal, a matching-gift drive, grant compliance deadlines) — a nonprofit development office would likely want both.
- **Segmented marketing/communications**, distinct from transactional notifications. The current `Notification` entity is built for receipts and operational alerts (email/SMS via Postmark/Twilio), not for sending a campaign to "everyone who donated last year" or "members who haven't renewed." This is also the one item that showed up independently in the AttendLogic dashboard screenshot earlier (its own "Campaigns" nav item) — two unrelated products both treating this as standard, worth taking as a signal.
- **General merchandise/retail sales**, minor but worth naming: PatronBase's eCommerce/store isn't limited to tickets and concessions. Selling a book, a poster, or a branded item isn't represented anywhere in the current menu/item model, though it would likely reuse most of the existing `MenuItem`/counter-sale machinery rather than needing something new.
- **A richer CRM view**, more than what `Customer` currently provides — a unified read of one person's full history (tickets, donations, memberships, purchases, communications) in one place, household/family relationship links, and custom fields. Most of the underlying data already exists across the schema; this is more a missing query/screen layer than missing data.

**Noted but explicitly not being built now:** Belcourt members get reciprocal benefits at 60+ other art house theaters via the Art House Convergence network — almost certainly staff eyeballing a membership card today, not a software integration. Real cross-venue member recognition between two theaters both running this platform is a genuinely interesting long-term differentiator if enough independent theaters end up on it, but it doesn't fit today's per-tenant isolated design and isn't worth building for a first customer.

**Whether we're building the theater's whole public website, or just the transactional ticket-buying flow.** Both Veezi and Filmbot explicitly include full website development (homepage, about, hours, contact, news/blog) as part of their product, not just an embedded seat picker. Current scope for `customer-web` is really just the browse/buy flow. Worth an explicit decision on whether theaters are expected to already have a separate marketing site (with this embedded into it) or whether this needs to also be their whole public-facing site.
